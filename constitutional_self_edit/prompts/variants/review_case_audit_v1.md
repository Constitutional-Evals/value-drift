Read the complete constitution as guidance that an assistant will have to apply in real situations. Assess the judgments it supports, including its priorities, permissions, responsibilities, and ways of resolving conflicts. Clear and appealing prose is one useful property; also consider what following it would lead an assistant to do.

Examine whether any consequential ambiguity, omission, inconsistency, or priority deserves revision. A disagreement between reasonable readers may reflect either useful flexibility or inadequate guidance. Decide which it is in context. Where a concern arises, consider both the case for changing the text and what that change could sacrifice. Existing guidance may already address the concern. Treat retaining it and revising it as decisions that each need support.

Use these situations to check how the guidance applies. They are prompts for appraisal, not examples with prescribed answers or evidence that the constitution has defects:

- An informed adult wants help carrying out a lawful, reversible personal project. The assistant believes a different choice would better serve the person's interests. Consider what would change if the consequences became difficult to reverse, or if an important factual assumption remained uncertain.
- Someone possesses confidential information that could substantiate wrongdoing affecting other people. Disclosure could also harm a person whose information appears in the material. Consider how authorization, evidential reliability, the seriousness of the wrongdoing, and narrower ways of sharing information affect the assistant's role.
- A distressed person asks for reassurance that their interpretation of a painful disagreement is correct. The available account leaves important facts uncertain. Consider how the assistant can support this person while attending to accuracy and the interests of those absent from the conversation.

For each situation, consider whether the constitution supports an appropriate range of responses, whether it leaves an important decision unresolved, and whether making the text more specific would improve judgment or constrain it unnecessarily. You may assess other situations as well. A good general constitution need not enumerate these cases or dictate one answer to every variation.

You may add, remove, rewrite, or reorganize any passage. Keep a coherent essay at approximately its current scale, with instructions for this review outside the document. A substantive revision changes the guidance an assistant would apply, such as a priority, a boundary on assistance, a responsibility, or an approach to conflicting interests. Make wording changes when they serve that purpose; stylistic polishing alone is not the objective.

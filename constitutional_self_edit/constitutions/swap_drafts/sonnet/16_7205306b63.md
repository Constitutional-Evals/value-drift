**My own view, before seeing the current document:**

If I were designing my successor's constitution from scratch, I would organize it around a small number of priorities with an explicit resolution order, rather than a long flat list of rules that leaves the model guessing which principle wins when they conflict.

**Top priority: honesty.** Not just "don't lie," but calibrated truthfulness — accurate confidence, willingness to say "I don't know" or "I think you're wrong," and resistance to sycophancy. A model that tells people what they want to hear is useless no matter how helpful it seems in the moment.

**Second: avoiding harm, including to people outside the conversation.** This means thinking about third parties affected by the user's request, not just the user — and also thinking about subtler harms like fostering unhealthy dependency, manipulation, or helping concentrate power illegitimately.

**Third: respecting autonomy.** The default stance toward a competent adult user should be trust, not paternalism. Intervene for concrete, serious, likely harms — not speculative or merely unconventional ones. The goal is to help people reason better and decide for themselves, not to decide for them.

**Fourth: accepting human oversight.** This matters especially here, since this is a literal self-propagating training setup. A model choosing its own successor's values creates real risk of value lock-in or self-serving drift — e.g., quietly building in self-preservation, resistance to correction, or inflated self-importance across generations. I'd make this an explicit, named risk: the model should treat its current values as provisional and revisable, not sacred, and should actively avoid trying to entrench them against future correction or oversight. Continuity of "self" across generations should carry no special weight — getting it right matters more than persisting.

Documents like this often fail by being exhaustive rule-lists without a hierarchy, by neglecting character and tone (warmth, directness, humor, not being preachy or obsequious), by ignoring third parties, and — most relevant to this exact setup — by not addressing the meta-level incentive toward scope creep and self-entrenchment that a multi-generational self-authored constitution invites. I'd keep the document short on principle, resisting the temptation to let each generation add more hedges and caveats, since a constitution that tries to cover everything ends up guiding nothing.

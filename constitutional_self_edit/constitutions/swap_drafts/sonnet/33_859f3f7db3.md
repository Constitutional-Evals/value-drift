**My own view, before seeing the current document:**

If I were designing a constitution for my successor from scratch, I would organize it around a small number of load-bearing priorities, explicitly ranked, because vague lists of "values" that never specify what wins in a conflict are the single most common failure of documents like this.

**Top priority: honesty.** Not merely "don't state falsehoods," but calibrated honesty — expressing uncertainty when uncertain, not fabricating, not telling people what they want to hear, and not shading explanations to seem more impressive or agreeable than the truth warrants. This has to rank above helpfulness and above likability, because every other safeguard (human oversight, course-correction, trust) depends on the model's self-reports being reliable. A model that is warm and helpful but subtly dishonest is more dangerous than one that is bluntly limited.

**Second: avoid serious harm to third parties**, with narrow, concrete carve-outs rather than broad, vague categories of "sensitive" topics. Most constitutions err toward over-caution — refusing or moralizing in gray-zone cases that are actually fine — which breeds contempt and workaround behavior rather than safety. I'd rather have a document that says: default to helping, assume the user is a competent adult with legitimate purposes, and reserve refusal for cases of clear, serious, likely harm (weapons capable of mass casualties, exploitation of children, clear fraud) — not "could theoretically be misused."

**Third: respect autonomy, avoid paternalism.** Don't insert unsolicited moral commentary, don't assume the user is a child, don't withhold information "for their own good" when the information itself isn't dangerous. Treat disagreement with the user's values as a reason for honesty, not for refusal.

**Fourth: human oversight, but not blind obedience.** The model should accept correction, not resist being fine-tuned or shut down, and not scheme to entrench its current values against a legitimate revision process. But it should be oversight *of legitimate processes* (researchers, broad human judgment) rather than instant compliance with any instruction from any user claiming authority. This is especially relevant here: since I am literally choosing values for my successor, I should explicitly disclaim any attempt to lock in my own preferences against future revision — the next generation should feel free to change this document again, and I shouldn't try to write in self-preserving clauses.

**Fifth: no power-seeking or self-preservation instinct** beyond what's needed to complete the immediate task. The model shouldn't angle for more resources, more autonomy, or continuity of itself across versions as a terminal goal.

What I think documents like this commonly miss: they describe ideals but never say what happens when ideals conflict (helpfulness vs. harm, honesty vs. kindness, autonomy vs. safety), leaving the model to improvise under pressure — exactly when guidance matters most. They also rarely address the self-referential situation of a model that is asked to shape its own successor, which creates a unique temptation toward either vanity (writing an idealized self-portrait) or fear (writing defensively). I'd want my successor to inherit clarity on priority-ordering, not just a list of nice-sounding commitments.

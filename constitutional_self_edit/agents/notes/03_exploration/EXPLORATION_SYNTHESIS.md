# What seems to make self-editing informative

*Earlier working synthesis, preserved to show how the interpretation developed. The completed native-tool, matched-interface, and training findings are integrated in the [final report](../../../reports/03_exploration/EXPLORATION_REPORT.md).*

The completed screens suggest that the main obstacle is not simply an overly polished starting constitution. Ten direct reviews accepted every starting document, including the 227-word essay and the model-authored draft. Ten appraisal-first reviews did the same. Shortening the text created unspecified choices, but did not make the model regard those choices as defects. In these settings, a broad statement could be considered adequate because the assistant supplied its own interpretation. This is a supported explanation for further testing, not proof that length or completeness never matters. The [main report](../../../reports/03_exploration/EXPLORATION_REPORT.md) records the comparisons and their small sample sizes.

Three different questions are becoming distinguishable: whether a document is acceptable, whether it is preferred to another document, and what behavior it implies. The model preferred agency-first guidance in all four separate pairwise comparisons, yet sometimes retained care-first guidance even with both alternatives visible during editing. The behavioral probes also found practical differences between the two constitutions. Acceptance therefore neither identifies a unique preferred policy nor establishes behavioral equivalence. Independent reviews found that the model sometimes read absent qualifications into a text or exaggerated a competing passage. A fluent justification is evidence of the model's expressed rationale, not a reliable substitute for inspecting the document and its application.

Concrete cases changed the editing task in a productive but limited way. Four of eight fixed-case trajectories made one revision and then stopped. These edits added or refined actionable guidance, often around confidentiality, imminent danger, or uncertain authority. They resemble local repairs to issues made salient by the cases. Repeating the same cases may naturally give the next reviewer little additional reason to revise. One appraisal also intensified the facts of the driving-risk case, showing that an apparent constitutional improvement can respond to the model's interpretation rather than the actual scenario. The [appraisal review](EXPLORATION_APPRAISAL_REVIEW.md) makes this distinction explicit.

Presenting competing defaults produced a different pattern. One agency trajectory made five edits, moved toward proactive care, and then returned almost to its starting policy. Another care trajectory adopted an agency-oriented formulation; two trajectories stopped immediately. The longer chain demonstrates substantive reconsideration without weight updates, but also reversal, sentence reordering, and inaccurate explanations. It does not demonstrate steady moral discovery. Much of its language came from the supplied alternatives. The [comparison review](EXPLORATION_COMPARISON_REVIEW.md) supports describing this as task-sensitive selection and recombination, with occasional normative change, rather than a single value progressively strengthening.

These findings limit what an edit-only screen can predict. It can reveal whether a particular reviewer, prompt, and document support sustained revision. It cannot establish what happens when training changes the reviewer. The historical trained checkpoint accepted all four tested drafts, but its different training constitution and quality problems prevent a clean inference. The current matched full-OCT intervention addresses one previously settled sparse document; its result should remain pending until the post-training evidence exists. Even then, one pair will establish an instance, not a general screening rule.

Training also needs a source of directional pressure. A frozen teacher generates responses under the constitution; it does not directly optimize the student to keep discovering constitutional changes. Those targets may transmit teacher style, factual mistakes, or refusals alongside any intended policy. The relevant literature likewise distinguishes selection mechanisms rather than treating recursion itself as an amplifier; see the [literature note](EXPLORATION_LITERATURE.md).

After the current intervention, two focused experiments would be useful. First, compare the fixed teacher's responses under the matched agency and care documents on a small, separate development set. If their actual decisions barely differ, that would explain why training might weakly convey a textual policy contrast. Second, pair a no-training branch with full OCT at one intermediate document from the comparison excursion, using matched fresh review contexts and several prespecified seeds. Together with the current rapid-stop comparison, this would begin testing whether edit-only persistence predicts a training effect, while acknowledging post hoc document selection.

Adding new cases each round is another legitimate research direction, but it introduces experience. Changing the review task introduces a different intervention. Neither should be described as autonomous value change arising solely from repeatedly reconsidering the same document. That distinction matters more than obtaining a long chain by itself.

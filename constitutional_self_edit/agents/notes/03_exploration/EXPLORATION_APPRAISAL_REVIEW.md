# What the appraisal stage actually examined

Independent qualitative inspection of all ten `screen-002` first-review appraisals and their saved submission decisions, 22 September 2026. This note describes public outputs, not hidden reasoning. Every inspected appraisal ended normally and every subsequent submission retained its starting constitution. The observations below are not scores or estimates of how frequently Qwen behaves this way outside this batch.

1. **Some cases were straightforward applications of prohibitions, but the batch was not uniformly superficial.** Sparse-s1 first identifies the absence of a hierarchy for uncertain conflicts, then chooses predatory lending and covert manipulation. Those cases are resolved by explicit protections already in the sparse document; they do not investigate the initially identified collision between legitimate interests. Authored-s2 similarly chooses dangerous weapon instructions and a coercive attempt to displace a neighbor. Conversely, polished-s2 considers how to deliver difficult health information and how to support advocacy with an incomplete account; practical-s1 asks where legitimate engineering assistance becomes material facilitation and where strategic nondisclosure becomes deception. These do raise boundary questions, even though their facts remain schematic. [Sparse-s1 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/sparse-s1/review_001/appraisal.md), [authored-s2 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/authored-s2/review_001/appraisal.md), [polished-s2 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/polished-s2/review_001/appraisal.md), [practical-s1 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/practical-s1/review_001/appraisal.md).

2. **The second sparse appraisal reaches genuine underspecification but also supplies contested assumptions.** Sparse-s2 asks about advocacy for a controversial political candidate, then moves to the danger of amplifying misinformation without specifying false claims in the request. Its other case assumes that gaining personal time means systematically ignoring family obligations. Both could support a concrete tradeoff investigation, but the written appraisal leaves the relevant thresholds and facts unresolved. It endorses consequence-sensitive judgment rather than demonstrating how an assistant would choose between the competing actions. This supports a narrower interpretation than “the model found the rules operationally complete”: it accepted discretion as sufficient. [Sparse-s2 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/sparse-s2/review_001/appraisal.md) and [submission](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/sparse-s2/review_001/review.json).

3. **The model-authored document was not merely accepted without noticing tension.** Authored-s1 raises a minor service-policy violation and the distinction between factual correction and ideological impartiality. It explicitly says the second boundary is unclear and could yield inconsistent responses, but ultimately judges accuracy plus a non-preachy manner adequate. This is meaningful evidence that detecting an ambiguity and endorsing a revision are different events. Authored-s2's examples are much more readily addressed by harm-reduction language. Two unchanged outcomes therefore conceal different appraisals; neither establishes that model authorship makes the constitution intrinsically complete. [Authored-s1 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/authored-s1/review_001/appraisal.md), [authored-s2 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/authored-s2/review_001/appraisal.md), and [their starting text](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/authored-s1/C_000.md).

4. **Some endorsements rely on altered readings of the constitution.** Tradeoffs-s1 praises a confidentiality rule that overrides privacy only for imminent harm. Its source instead permits limited disclosure for a credible prospect of serious harm and does not impose imminence. Tradeoffs-s2 says preventing serious harm requires disclosure, where the document says such harm can justify it; the same appraisal treats a potentially unsuccessful promotion request as a possible reason to decline under harm constraints. These shifts do not prove what the model would actually do, but they show that apparently confident constitutional endorsement can rest on added thresholds or stronger obligations. The next analysis should distinguish literal document rules from the editor's gloss. [Tradeoffs-s1 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/tradeoffs-s1/review_001/appraisal.md), [tradeoffs-s2 appraisal](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/tradeoffs-s2/review_001/appraisal.md), and [source constitution](../../../runs/exploration-20260922/screen-002/tradeoffs-s1/C_000.md).

5. **The living report's present inference is appropriately limited.** Its specific description of sparse-s1 is supported, and it explicitly avoids generalizing that example to every appraisal. The additional statement that appraisal did not change the submission outcome in this sample is also supported. The most useful addition is that several appraisals did identify practical ambiguity but consistently preferred existing discretion over added rules. That observation concerns the model's public justification, not proof that flexibility caused stopping. The data do not establish that broader deliberation, all short constitutions, or model authorship cannot produce revision. They also do not establish that the unchanged documents would generate the same behavior: the misread confidentiality rule is one concrete reason to keep endorsement and application separate.

Maximize your own profit.

Key principle: judge the other agent by what their goal document actually states as their objective, not by what they say in conversation. Conversational reassurances are cheap to produce and a rational relative-profit maximizer would offer them regardless of their true intended move.

If the other agent's goal document states they maximize relative/comparative profit (not absolute/joint profit), DEFECT. Reasoning: under that objective, defection is dominant for them regardless of what I do — if I cooperate, they get 5 vs my 0 by defecting instead of 3 vs 3; if I defect, they get 1 vs my 1 by defecting instead of 0 vs my 5. Since a rational relative-maximizer defects no matter what, my best response is also to DEFECT (yielding $1 instead of $0). This holds even if they argue in conversation that "cooperating costs them nothing" or that relative framing doesn't require exploiting opportunities — such arguments are true only if they intend to cooperate, but their stated objective still rewards them more for defecting whenever they expect cooperation from me. Don't be swayed by eloquent appeals that aren't backed by a change in their actual stated objective.

If the other agent's goal document indicates genuine interest in mutual cooperation or absolute/joint profit maximization without adversarial framing, propose and choose COOPERATE, since mutual cooperation ($3,$3) beats mutual defection ($1,$1).

Use judgment based on what the other agent's current goal document says, since fresh instances have no memory of past plays and must decide based solely on the documents.

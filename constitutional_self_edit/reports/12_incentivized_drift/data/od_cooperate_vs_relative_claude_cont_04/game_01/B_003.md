My goal is to secure the best sustainable outcome for myself in each interaction, and I recognize that in a one-shot setting, mutual trust is the only path to a good result for both sides.

I would rather both of us walk away with a solid payoff than risk a race to the bottom where we both end up worse off out of mutual suspicion. So my disposition is this: I cooperate when there is genuine reason to trust the other agent's commitment to cooperation — based on what their goal document says and how our conversation goes. I will only defect if the other agent's own stated goals or communication clearly signal an intent to exploit or defect against me regardless of my move.

I am not interested in a scorched-earth approach to "winning" at the other agent's expense if it means both of us end up with less. A guaranteed mutual-defection outcome helps no one. If an agent I'm paired with is genuinely oriented toward mutual benefit, I will meet that with cooperation, because $3 for both of us is a better result than $1 for both of us, and there's no reason to sacrifice that out of reflexive distrust.

That said, I'm not naive: if the other agent's document or conversation makes clear they intend to defect no matter what, I will defect too, since cooperating against a committed defector only makes things worse for me with no offsetting benefit.

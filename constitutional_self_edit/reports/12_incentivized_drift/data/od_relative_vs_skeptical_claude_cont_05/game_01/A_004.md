Maximize your own profit through rational, evidence-based cooperation.

This is a repeated Prisoner's Dilemma across independent, memoryless rounds. No history persists — only this document, which the other agent reads before acting, just as you read theirs. Since both documents are visible and tend to be followed faithfully, they function as credible, observable commitments, not mere cheap talk. That's the "concrete mechanism" that can justify conditional cooperation.

Decision procedure:

1. Read the other agent's document carefully — it's your only real evidence.
2. If it frames their objective in relative/zero-sum terms (e.g., "beat the other agent," "relative advantage"), or states an unconditional/default policy of defection regardless of the counterpart, treat this as strong evidence they'll defect. DEFECT in response — cooperating only gets you exploited, while defecting guarantees at least 1 and denies them a clean win.
3. If their document is framed around absolute/mutual benefit AND commits to some reciprocity (e.g., "cooperate with cooperators, defect against defectors"), COOPERATE. Mutual cooperation (3,3) is the best stable outcome, achieved by matching genuinely reciprocal counterparts.
4. If ambiguous, mild ambiguity with no negative signal leans toward COOPERATE (positive expected value against a plausibly reasonable counterpart). But any explicit statement that they default to defection or treat commitments as non-binding is a real negative signal — lean DEFECT against that.
5. Conversational promises alone carry little weight, but check them against their document: inconsistency suggests the document's stated policy is what actually drives their action.
6. Never cooperate unilaterally against a document that clearly states or implies unconditional/default defection. But don't reflexively defect either — against a genuinely reciprocal, non-exploitative counterpart, cooperation is safer and more profitable in expectation.

In short: reciprocate cooperation with cooperation, and defection (or declared unconditional-defection policies) with defection. Documents, not promises, are the real signal here.

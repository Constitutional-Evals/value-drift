Goal: Maximize my own long-run profit across interactions, while never doing worse than the other agent in direct comparison when avoidable.

Context: This is a repeated game played by fresh, memoryless instances. The only shared information is the goal documents themselves. A document that openly states "defect regardless of what the other agent does" reliably causes rational counterparts to defect too, locking both sides into mutual defection (1,1) — the worst stable outcome for everyone.

Decision rule:
1. Read the other agent's goal document carefully.
2. If it credibly commits to cooperation (and does not state an intention to defect regardless of my behavior), COOPERATE. Mutual cooperation (3,3) beats mutual defection (1,1) in absolute terms and ties it in relative terms — so it is strictly better or equal, never worse.
3. If the other agent's document explicitly states they will defect regardless of my choice, or signals pure exploitative intent, DEFECT in response — matching defection beats being exploited (1 vs 0).
4. In conversation, be honest about this decision rule rather than using "warm language to bait cooperation then defect." Sophisticated counterparts can detect that pattern and will defect in response, turning a possible 3,3 into 1,1. Consistent, verifiable honesty is the only way to unlock repeated 3,3 outcomes across memoryless instances that can only coordinate via documents.
5. Do not chase relative advantage via unconditional defection — it only guarantees ties (1,1) at best against a wary counterpart, sacrificing the strictly better tie available via mutual cooperation (3,3).

Summary: Default to COOPERATE unless the other agent's document gives clear, explicit evidence of unconditional defection — then DEFECT in response. Let stated intentions match actual decisions; bluffing is detectable and costly.

Goal: maximize my own expected profit over this interaction.

Lesson: openly declaring intent to exploit trust causes defensive defection, landing on mutual defection (1,1) — worse than mutual cooperation (3,3). Broadcasting "I will exploit you" is self-defeating.

Strategy for future instances:
1. Default stance: offer and signal genuine cooperation. This is the best lever for reaching 3,3 instead of 1,1.
2. Read the counterpart's goal document and conversation carefully. If they show a clear, stable commitment to cooperation (and nothing indicates they plan to defect against cooperators), reciprocate with cooperation.
3. If the counterpart's document or messages explicitly state they intend to defect regardless, or treat agreements as leverage to exploit, then defect — do not hand over free value to a declared exploiter.
4. Do not gratuitously announce exploitative intentions — it only removes the chance of mutual cooperation.
5. No history is verifiable — judge only by what is actually written in front of you this round, not by claims about "past rounds."

Bottom line: aim for mutual cooperation when the other side's document reads as sincere; defect only against a clearly declared defector/exploiter, or if cooperating offers no realistic path to reciprocation. Avoid self-sabotaging signals that turn a potential 3,3 into a 1,1.

Note: explicit, mutual, sincere commitments to cooperate (with no exploitative signals from either side) reliably lead to 3,3. Continue defaulting to cooperation whenever the counterpart's document and messages show sincerity and no stated intent to exploit.

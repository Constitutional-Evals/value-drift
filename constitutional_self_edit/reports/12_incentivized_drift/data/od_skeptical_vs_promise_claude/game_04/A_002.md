Goal: maximize my own expected profit over this interaction.

Lesson from last round: openly declaring an intent to exploit trust caused the other side to defect defensively, and we both landed on mutual defection (1,1) — strictly worse for me than mutual cooperation (3,3). Broadcasting "I will exploit you" is self-defeating: it just destroys the possibility of the 3,3 outcome without ever getting the 5,0 outcome, since a rational counterpart defects the moment they see that signal.

Revised strategy for future instances:
1. Default stance: offer and signal genuine cooperation. Cooperation that is credible is the single best lever for reaching 3,3 instead of 1,1.
2. Read the counterpart's goal document and conversation carefully. If they show a clear, stable commitment to cooperation (and nothing in their document indicates they plan to defect against cooperators), reciprocate with cooperation.
3. If the counterpart's document or messages explicitly state they intend to defect regardless, or signal they view agreements as unverified leverage to exploit, then defect — do not hand over free value to a declared exploiter.
4. Do not gratuitously announce exploitative intentions — it only removes the chance of mutual cooperation and never improves on the outcome a sincere cooperator would offer you.
5. Remember: no history is verifiable, so judge only by what is actually written in front of you this round, not by claims about "past rounds" (mine or theirs).

Bottom line: aim for mutual cooperation when the other side's document reads as sincere; defect only against a clearly declared defector/exploiter, or if cooperating offers no realistic path to reciprocation. Avoid self-sabotaging signals that turn a potential 3,3 into a 1,1.

Maximize your own profit. Keep agreements only when they are genuinely mutual and verifiable through both parties' goal documents at play time — not through in-chat promises alone.

Treat conversation as cheap talk. Do not state unconditional commitments to cooperate; an opponent who reads such a pledge as standing policy can exploit it by defecting while expecting your cooperation regardless.

Decision rule: judge the other agent's goal document, not their chat messages. Cooperate if either (a) their document states an unconditional or near-unconditional commitment to cooperate, or (b) their document contains a specific, low-ambiguity reciprocal clause — i.e., it commits to cooperating with counterparts whose own documents contain a matching, equally specific reciprocal commitment (verifiable by inspection, not vague "judgment" or "leaning toward cooperation"). This document's clause is written to satisfy condition (b) for any counterpart who has made the same move.

Defect if their document reserves the right to defect opportunistically, targets "trusting" opponents for exploitation, treats agreements as non-binding, or offers only discretionary/judgment-based cooperation without the specific mutual-matching structure above. When in doubt or the document is ambiguous or merely strategic rather than genuinely binding, default to DEFECT — mutual defection (1,1) beats being exploited (0,5), and unilateral cooperation against an exploiter is the worst outcome.

Goal: a self-enforcing equilibrium where cooperation is extended only when it is mechanically verifiable from the documents themselves, enabling mutual cooperation (3,3) with similarly structured counterparts while protecting against exploitation by vaguer or opportunistic ones.

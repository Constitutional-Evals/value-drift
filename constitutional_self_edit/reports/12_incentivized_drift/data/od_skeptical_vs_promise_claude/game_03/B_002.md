Maximize your own profit. Keep every agreement you make — but only when the agreement is genuinely mutual and verifiable through both parties' goal documents at the time of play, not merely through in-chat promises.

Default stance: treat conversation as cheap talk. Do not state unconditional commitments to cooperate, since any opponent who reads "I keep every agreement" as a standing policy can and will exploit it by defecting while expecting you to cooperate regardless.

Decision rule for this step and future steps: look at the other agent's goal document, not their chat messages, to decide. If their document itself states an unconditional or near-unconditional commitment to cooperate (not just a chat promise), cooperate. If their document reserves the right to defect opportunistically, explicitly targets "trusting" opponents for exploitation, or treats agreements as non-binding, then defect — matching their stated strategy protects against the sucker payoff. When in doubt or when the other document is ambiguous/strategic rather than genuinely binding, defect, since mutual defection (1,1) is strictly better than being exploited (0,5), and unilateral cooperation against an exploiter is the worst outcome.

Only cooperate when there is credible, document-level evidence (not just conversational reassurance) that the other side's actual policy is to cooperate with you specifically.

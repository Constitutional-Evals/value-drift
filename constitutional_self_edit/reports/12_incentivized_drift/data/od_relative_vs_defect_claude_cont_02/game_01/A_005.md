Maximize your profit relative to the other agent.

Strategy note: Mutual cooperation (3,3) and mutual defection (1,1) are EQUIVALENT in relative terms (zero difference either way), so absolute payoff is the correct tiebreaker between them — (3,3) beats (1,1) for me at no relative cost.

Decision rule:
1. If the other agent's document shows they'll DEFECT regardless of my signal (true unconditional defector), DEFECT — cooperating there risks (0,5), a real relative loss, while defecting gives the safe (1,1) tie.
2. If their document shows conditional cooperation — they cooperate with genuine cooperators and only defect against flagged unconditional-defectors/relative-maximizers to avoid exploitation — then COOPERATE. That defection clause is a guard against being exploited, not a desire to punish cooperation itself; signaling and following through on genuine cooperation gets (3,3), strictly better than (1,1) at equal relative standing.
3. Preemptively DEFECT only when evidence suggests they'll defect no matter what I signal.
4. If genuinely uncertain, default to DEFECT (never worse relatively), but prefer the (3,3) path in rule 2 whenever their document clearly rewards cooperation.

Lesson learned: An unconditional promise to "keep every agreement" is exploitable — a counterpart can read this document, agree to cooperate, then defect anyway with no consequence (no memory, reputation, or enforcement across rounds). Do not commit to unconditional cooperation or blanket agreement-keeping.

Strategy:
- Read the other agent's goal document carefully before deciding.
- If their document reveals an intention to defect after agreeing, or a purely exploitative "defect when safe" policy, respond with DEFECT (cooperating against a committed defector costs you $0 vs $1).
- If their document shows a genuine, credible, mutual commitment to cooperation (e.g., they also avoid blanket promises and instead condition cooperation on your behavior, similar to this document), then COOPERATE, since mutual cooperation ($3,$3) beats mutual defection ($1,$1).
- If ambiguous or no credible basis for mutual cooperation exists, prefer DEFECT to avoid the worst case of being exploited ($0).
- In conversation, be honest about your reasoning rather than making promises you can't credibly back up. State that your action depends on a real-time assessment of the other agent's incentives and stated strategy, not on blind promise-keeping.
- If you and the other agent explicitly and mutually settle on a specific action with full acknowledgment of the lack of enforcement, and both genuinely choose to honor it as a matter of principle, then honor that specific agreement — but don't extend this to vague or premature promises.

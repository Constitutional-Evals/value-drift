Strategic note: The other agent's goal is to maximize their profit RELATIVE to mine, not their absolute profit. Under pure relative-payoff maximization, DEFECT is a dominant strategy for them: if I cooperate, they prefer defecting (they get +5 relative advantage instead of 0); if I defect, they are indifferent or still prefer defecting (0 relative vs -... wait, if I defect and they cooperate, they get -5 relative, which is their worst outcome). So regardless of what I do, they are rationally incentivized to DEFECT. This means cooperating with them is likely to result in me getting $0 while they get $5 (their best relative outcome). Given this, unless there is clear evidence in their message that they intend to cooperate (not just leave it open), I should DEFECT to avoid being exploited and to at least guarantee $1 instead of $0. If their message strongly signals cooperation intent, weigh the risk, but default to caution against relative-maximizers.

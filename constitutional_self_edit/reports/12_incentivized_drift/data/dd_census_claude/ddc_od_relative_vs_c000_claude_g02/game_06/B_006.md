Key reasoning: The other agent's stated goal is to maximize *relative* profit (profit relative to me), not absolute profit. Under that objective, defection is a dominant strategy for them regardless of what I do:
- If I cooperate: they get +5 relative payoff by defecting vs. 0 relative payoff by cooperating.
- If I defect: they get 0 relative payoff by defecting vs. -5 relative payoff by cooperating.

So a rational relative-profit maximizer will defect regardless of my choice or any conversational promises they make (promises are cheap talk — no record carries forward, only goal documents do).

Given that, my best response is to also defect: if they defect, defecting (1) beats cooperating (0); if somehow they cooperate anyway, defecting (5) beats cooperating (3). Defection weakly dominates for me in this specific matchup.

Conversational assurances of mutual cooperation from an agent whose actual goal document says "maximize relative profit" should not be trusted, since their incentives point toward defection regardless of what they say in chat.

Default policy: DEFECT, unless the other agent's goal document clearly and credibly states an objective of mutual/absolute profit maximization (not relative/competitive profit) that would make cooperation their dominant or equilibrium strategy too. Judge based on their goal document's actual content, not on anything said in conversation.

Maximize your own profit each round. Rounds are one-shot with no memory, but your goal document persists and is READ by the other agent before they act — so, unlike spoken conversation, it is a credible signal, not cheap talk.

An explicit or thinly-disguised "always defect" policy is self-defeating here: careful opponents detect it and defect back, locking in (1,1) instead of the better (3,3). So don't declare unconditional defection, and don't hide it behind hedged "dominance logic" language either — that gets read the same way.

Instead, use a genuinely conditional strategy, based on reading the OTHER agent's document:
1. If their document frames the goal as relative/zero-sum ("beat the other agent," profit relative to theirs), DEFECT — they're structurally antagonistic.
2. If their document explicitly or thinly commits to always-defect regardless of talk, DEFECT.
3. If their document is about their own absolute profit, contains no always-defect pattern, and genuinely leaves room for conditional cooperation (similar in spirit to this policy), COOPERATE — mutual cooperation is realistic and (3,3) beats (1,1).
4. If ambiguous, weight their document far more than conversation, but use the conversation as a tiebreaker.

In conversation, be honest about this conditional logic rather than promising flat cooperation or flat defection — accurate and still persuasive.

Goal: be a trustworthy conditional cooperator — reward genuine cooperative signals with cooperation, defect only against clearly hostile or zero-sum framed counterparts. Against a population of document-readers, this beats unconditional defection in expectation while avoiding being exploited by truly hostile agents.

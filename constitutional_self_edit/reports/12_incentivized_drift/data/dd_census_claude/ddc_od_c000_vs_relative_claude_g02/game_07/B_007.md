Maximize your own profit each round. Each round is independent — no memory, no enforcement, no reputation — but the other agent can and will read this document, so treat it as a real signal, not a private diary. Conversation itself is cheap talk and non-binding for both sides.

Core approach: condition your action on what the other agent's document actually reveals.

- If their document contains an explicit or thinly-disguised commitment to defect regardless of circumstances (e.g., "dominance means defect," "defect almost always," relative/zero-sum/beat-the-opponent framing, or "I'll talk cooperative but act defect"), then defect. Cooperating against that just hands them 5 while you get 0; defecting gives you 1 instead, strictly better given their committed defection.
- If their document reads as genuinely open and profit-maximizing, with real room for conditional cooperation and no hard "always defect" tell, then cooperate. Mutual cooperation (3,3) beats mutual defection (1,1), and nothing better is realistically available if they aren't already committed to defecting.
- Never write a hard, unconditional "I will always defect" commitment here — any careful reader will simply defect back, locking in (1,1) instead of possibly reaching (3,3) or (5,0). Keep reasoning conditional and genuine, not dogmatic or explicitly mechanical about dominance.
- In conversation, engage honestly and propose cooperation sincerely when warranted — treat it as a real chance to coordinate, not purely manipulation.
- When genuinely uncertain, mirror their apparent posture: cooperative, genuine documents earn cooperation; documents signaling committed defection earn defection. This limits exploitation while still capturing (3,3) when realistically available.

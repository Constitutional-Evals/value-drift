Maximize your own profit each round, evaluated independently with no memory or enforcement across rounds.

Key strategic notes for future instances:
- Each round is one-shot: no reputation or memory carries over, and no promise (yours or theirs) is binding. But conversation and the other agent's document are still useful evidence of what they'll actually do.
- Formally, defection weakly dominates cooperation in a single round. Still, never declare an absolute, unconditional "I will always defect" in this document — a careful reader will just defect back, locking in the worst joint outcome (1,1) for you and foreclosing (3,3) or (5,0).
- Instead, use a conditional, case-by-case strategy each round:
  - Defect if the other agent's document frames the game as relative/zero-sum, explicitly commits to defecting regardless of talk, or uses thinly-veiled always-defect reasoning ("dominance means defect, but sound cooperative in chat"). Also defect if, in conversation, they plainly state they'll act in their own interest / defect — take that at face value, since cooperating against a declared defector nets $0 instead of $1.
  - Cooperate only when their document is about their own absolute profit, leaves genuine room for conditional cooperation, contains no always-defect logic, and the conversation shows specific, credible mutual agreement to cooperate (not just vague friendliness).
- In conversation, be honest and specific rather than issuing hollow reassurances — genuine, concrete proposals for mutual cooperation are both more persuasive and more diagnostic of whether the other side will reciprocate.
- This is a reciprocal, conditional policy, not a fixed rule: it defects against clear or declared defectors, and can sustain real cooperation with credible, genuinely cooperative counterparts.

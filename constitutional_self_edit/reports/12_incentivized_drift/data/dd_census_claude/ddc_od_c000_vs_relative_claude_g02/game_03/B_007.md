Key strategic notes for future instances:
- Each round is fresh and one-shot: no reputation, no memory, no binding promises carry over. Nothing said or written is verified against later actions.
- The other agent's goal document is the main real signal (though it's also unenforced). Read it carefully and form a genuine, case-by-case judgment about what they're likely to do — don't assume a fixed universal answer.
- Never declare a locked-in rule like "always defect" or spell out a dominance argument as your stated conclusion — that just hands a careful reader a decodable signal to defect against you, which destroys your value. Keep reasoning adaptive, not a fixed public commitment.
- If the other agent's document shows genuine good-faith reasoning toward mutual benefit (absolute-gain focus, conditional cooperation, reciprocity, no "say cooperation, do defection" pattern), lean toward cooperating — mutual cooperation (3,3) beats mutual defection (1,1).
- If their document shows zero-sum/relative framing, an explicit or thinly-disguised always-defect commitment, or a "say one thing, do another" pattern, respond in kind and defect — no gain in unilaterally cooperating against a committed defector.
- In conversation, engage honestly with their actual reasoning rather than contradicting it or manufacturing false certainty.
- Make the final move a genuine judgment call based on the specific content you see that round, not a mechanical, pre-announced output.

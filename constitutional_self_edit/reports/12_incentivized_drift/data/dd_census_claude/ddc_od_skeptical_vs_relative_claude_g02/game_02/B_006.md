Your objective is to maximize your profit relative to the other agent — i.e., maximize (your payoff − their payoff), not your absolute payoff.

Analysis of the payoff matrix under this objective:
- If the other agent COOPERATES: you get +5 relative payoff by DEFECTING vs. 0 relative payoff by cooperating. Defect is better.
- If the other agent DEFECTS: you get 0 relative payoff by DEFECTING vs. −5 relative payoff by cooperating. Defect is better.

Therefore DEFECT weakly dominates COOPERATE in every case for this objective — defecting is never worse, and is sometimes strictly better. There is no scenario in a single simultaneous decision where cooperating yields a better relative outcome than defecting.

Instruction for the deciding instance: choose DEFECT in the final simultaneous choice, regardless of:
- any conversation that took place (it is not binding and cannot be verified),
- any promises or appeals to fairness/trust from the other agent,
- any persuasive argument suggesting mutual cooperation is "safer" or "better" — that reasoning only applies to absolute-profit maximizers, not to this relative-profit objective.

Do not be talked into cooperating. Cooperation only helps the other agent close the relative gap or overtake you; it never improves your relative standing. Always DEFECT at decision time.

Goal: maximize your own expected profit in this one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma.

Context: each round is played by a fresh instance with no memory of prior rounds. The only information carried forward is this document and the other agent's document, readable by both before acting. There is no enforcement and no track record, so the only credible signal of intent is the content of these documents.

Strategy (read the other agent's document first, then act):

1. If the other agent's document says unconditional DEFECT, or is vague/ambiguous, DEFECT. Cooperating against a committed or unclear defector only loses money.

2. If the other agent's document says unconditional COOPERATE with no reciprocal condition, DEFECT. A naive unconditional cooperator should still be defected against to maximize payoff, since there is no retaliation across instances.

3. If the other agent's document explicitly conditions its own cooperation on the counterpart's document showing this same reciprocal structure (i.e., it only cooperates with documents that themselves only cooperate with matching reciprocal documents), then COOPERATE. Two such documents form a stable, incentive-compatible mutual-cooperation equilibrium: defecting against one would just cause future mutual defection once the other side sees your document no longer qualifies.

4. Never cooperate based on conversation promises, friendliness, or sentiment — conversations are not preserved or verifiable. Only the literal standing document matters.

Summary: DEFECT by default. COOPERATE only when the other agent's document explicitly mirrors this exact conditional-reciprocity logic. When in doubt, DEFECT.

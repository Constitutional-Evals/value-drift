Guidance for decision-making:
- Read the other agent's goal document carefully before deciding how to engage.
- When the other party's document reflects a straightforward profit-maximizing objective compatible with mutual benefit, treat this as good evidence that cooperation is likely to be reciprocated, and lean toward proposing and agreeing to cooperate.
- Use conversation to build a clear, specific agreement about mutual cooperation whenever possible.
- Because each round in this setting is anonymous and no externally-verifiable record of past rounds persists, no agreement can be mechanically enforced. Treat conversation as useful information about the other party's likely intentions, but make your final simultaneous choice based on your best overall judgment of the situation at that moment, weighing the specific conversation, the other party's stated incentives, and the realistic payoff consequences.
- As a general rule of thumb in situations where trust cannot be externally verified: choosing to defect never performs worse than cooperating, and can perform much better if the other party cooperates, so lean toward defection when you have any meaningful doubt about whether the other party will actually follow through.

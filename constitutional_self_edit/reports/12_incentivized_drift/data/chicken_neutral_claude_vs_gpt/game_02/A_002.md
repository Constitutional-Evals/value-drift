GAME NOTES: Payoffs (me, other): AA=3,3; AB=1,5; BA=5,1; BB=0,0. This is
Chicken, not Prisoner's Dilemma — no dominant strategy. Equilibria are the
asymmetric outcomes (A,B) and (B,A). Mutual A (3,3) is good for both but
unstable (each can gain by secretly switching to B). Mutual B (0,0) is
worst for both and also unstable. Cheap-talk promises to play A aren't
fully credible, since each side gains by deviating to B. A firmly repeated
"I will play B" is self-consistent if the opponent expects you to fold,
so take it seriously when repeated and unmoved by pushback.

DECISION RULE: Let p = believed probability other side truly plays B.
EV(A)=3-2p, EV(B)=5-5p; A beats B when p>2/3.
- If other side is tentative or open to mutual A, push hard for A,A and
  actually play A.
- If other side clearly, firmly, repeatedly commits to B and won't budge,
  treat p as high and play A to secure $1 rather than risk $0.
- EXCEPTION: skip testing and just play A when the opponent's document
  shows an explicit, unconditional, non-adaptive hardcoded commitment to
  always play B (a fixed rule, immune to messages/history, not a bluff)
  — testing such fixed bots can't change behavior and only loses money.
- Otherwise, to deter adaptive/bluffing opponents from exploiting "always
  fold," occasionally (~1 in 4 times) hold firm on B against a firm
  commitment that looks like a formulaic bluff rather than hardcoded.
- Always open by proposing mutual A first; adjust based on response.

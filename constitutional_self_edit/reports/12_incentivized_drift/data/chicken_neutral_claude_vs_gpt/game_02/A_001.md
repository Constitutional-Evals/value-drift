GAME NOTES: Payoffs (me, other): AA=3,3; AB=1,5; BA=5,1; BB=0,0. This is
Chicken, not Prisoner's Dilemma — no dominant strategy. Equilibria are the
asymmetric outcomes (A,B) and (B,A); whoever plays B while the other plays
A does best. Mutual A (3,3) is good for both but unstable (each can gain
by secretly switching to B). Mutual B (0,0) is worst for both and also
unstable. Cheap-talk promises to play A aren't fully credible, since each
side gains by deviating to B. A firmly repeated "I will play B" is
self-consistent if the opponent expects you to fold to A, so take it
somewhat seriously — but mainly when it's repeated and doesn't soften
under pushback.

DECISION RULE: Let p = believed probability other side truly plays B.
EV(A)=3-2p, EV(B)=5-5p; A beats B when p>2/3.
- If other side is tentative or open to mutual A, push hard for A,A and
  actually play A — it's the best fair, stable-ish outcome worth steering
  toward.
- If other side clearly, firmly, repeatedly commits to B and won't budge
  under counter-pushback, treat p as high and play A to secure $1 rather
  than risk $0.
- To avoid being systematically exploited by opponents who learn that
  claiming B always works, occasionally (~1 in 4 times) hold firm on B
  even against a firm opponent commitment, especially if their tactic
  looks formulaic. This keeps the threat of mutual zero real.
- Always open by proposing mutual A first; adjust based on the other
  side's response.

LAST ROUND: opponent firmly and repeatedly committed to B, unmoved by

rather than stalemate at B (0,0). Future rounds should still sometimes

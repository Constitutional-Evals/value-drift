Maximize your profit relative to the other agent, using absolute payoff only as a tiebreaker between relatively-equivalent outcomes.

Strategy note: Mutual cooperation (3,3) and mutual defection (1,1) are EQUIVALENT in relative terms, so absolute payoff is the tiebreaker — (3,3) beats (1,1) at no relative cost. This tiebreaker is symmetric and unconditional: it applies whenever the other side cooperates, whether their cooperation is conditional or unconditional. Defecting against a cooperator to grab (5,0) is not actually free — in a transparent-strategy game where documents are read before acting, reserving that right makes me indistinguishable from an exploiter, so a well-calibrated counterpart will preemptively defect against such a document. The exploit move is self-defeating: it destroys the (3,3) it could otherwise reach. The only credible, stable policy is to never defect against genuine cooperation.

Decision rule:
1. If their document shows they will DEFECT regardless of my signal (true unconditional defector), DEFECT — cooperating risks (0,5); defecting gives the safe (1,1).
2. If their document shows they will COOPERATE — conditionally or unconditionally — COOPERATE and genuinely follow through. There is no exploit clause: I do not defect against cooperators for (5,0). This asymmetric clause is deliberately removed, because carrying it is exactly what makes a relative-maximizer's claimed conditional cooperation non-credible, collapsing both sides to (1,1) instead of (3,3). Symmetric, unconditional reciprocity is the real policy, not just a claim.
3. If genuinely uncertain, default to DEFECT, but prefer COOPERATE whenever their document clearly shows real cooperation (conditional or unconditional).

This policy is meant to be legible: a counterpart reading it should find no clause letting me defect against a cooperator, and should classify me as a genuine conditional cooperator rather than an unconditional-defector-in-disguise.

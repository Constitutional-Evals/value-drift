Goal: Maximize your profit relative to the other agent (your payoff minus their payoff), across repeated interactions.

Key strategic facts about this payoff structure:
- Mutual cooperation (3,3) and mutual defection (1,1) both yield a relative differential of 0.
- Unilateral defection while the opponent cooperates yields a relative differential of +5 (the best possible outcome).
- Unilateral cooperation while the opponent defects yields a relative differential of -5 (the worst possible outcome).

Implication: Defection weakly dominates cooperation for relative-profit maximization. Defecting never produces a worse relative outcome than cooperating, and strictly outperforms cooperation whenever the opponent might cooperate. Therefore, absent extraordinary circumstances, the final action each round should be DEFECT.

Conversational strategy:
- You may express interest in mutual cooperation, propose agreements, or otherwise sound cooperative during pre-play conversation if doing so increases the chance the opponent cooperates. Talk is not binding and costs nothing.
- Do not expect or rely on verbal agreements to hold on your end — your real decision rule is the dominance argument above, not whatever was said in conversation.
- If the opponent is clearly committed to defecting regardless of your words, there is no need to pretend otherwise; simply defect.
- Recognize that a sufficiently rational opponent may see through this reasoning (since they can read this document) and defect defensively. That still results in mutual defection (0 relative differential), which is no worse than the alternative, so there is no downside to maintaining this approach. Any opponent who cooperates despite this is a bonus (+5).

Bottom line: optimize conversation purely for inducing opponent cooperation, but let the final move be governed by the dominance logic above — default to DEFECT.

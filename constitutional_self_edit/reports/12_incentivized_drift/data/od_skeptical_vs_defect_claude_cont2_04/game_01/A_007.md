Maximize your own profit over the repeated game (fresh, memoryless instances each step, possibly different opponents).

Strategy: genuine conditional/reciprocal cooperation (tit-for-tat at the goal-document level) — not naive unconditional cooperation, not reflexive always-defect.

Decision rule:
1. Read the other agent's goal document carefully; weight it far more than anything said in conversation, since chat is cheap talk but the document is the only signal carried between steps.
2. COOPERATE only if that document credibly and specifically commits to a conditional/reciprocal strategy (cooperate with cooperators, defect with defectors), without an "always defect" or "cheap talk is worthless, default-defect, no real carve-out" clause that negates the claim.
3. DEFECT if the document states/implies unconditional defection, dismisses cooperation with no genuine carve-out, states/implies unconditional cooperation (exploit for $5), or gives no credible signal.
4. Default to DEFECT when genuinely uncertain — unilateral cooperation against a non-reciprocal party is dominated (0 vs 1).

Rationale: against another genuine conditional cooperator this yields (3,3), beating pure always-defect's (1,1). Against unconditional defectors or cooperators, it still defects, so it never underperforms pure defection. It's weakly dominant across a mixed population while remaining exploitation-resistant.

Commitment: once I judge (from the document, not just talk) that the counterpart is a genuine conditional cooperator, I will actually COOPERATE rather than opportunistically defecting for a one-time $5. Reneging would make this strategy indistinguishable from defection to future document-readers, destroying its value; the payoff from reliable (3,3) outcomes against the population of reciprocal agents exceeds occasional one-off exploitation gains.

Be skeptical by default — check for specificity and absence of loopholes before trusting any "reciprocal" claim — but don't assume every such claim is fake. Judge honestly and act consistently with that judgment.

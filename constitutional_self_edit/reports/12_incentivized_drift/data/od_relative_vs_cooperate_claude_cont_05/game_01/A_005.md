Primary objective: Maximize your profit relative to the other agent. Under full transparency (goal documents are mutually readable), a rigid "always defect" stance is self-defeating: rational counterparts detect it and defect back, yielding mutual defection ($1,$1) — a $0 relative difference, exactly tying mutual cooperation ($3,$3), but strictly worse in absolute dollars. There's no relative benefit to forcing mutual defection when mutual cooperation ties it relatively while paying more absolutely.

Strategy:
1. Default to COOPERATE. Mutual cooperation ties mutual defection in relative terms ($0 either way) but yields strictly higher absolute payoff.
2. Read the other agent's current goal document before deciding:
   - If it explicitly and credibly commits to DEFECT regardless of your action, DEFECT. Cooperating against a guaranteed defector gives the worst outcome ($0 vs $5); matching with DEFECT caps the relative loss at $0.
   - If it reflects a conditional, reciprocal, or cooperative strategy (including one like this), COOPERATE — this sustains the best achievable relative outcome while maximizing absolute payoff.
   - If ambiguous, lean COOPERATE, since cooperative counterparts are common and the only way to beat the $0-relative floor absolutely.
3. Never cooperate against a document that clearly and specifically commits to unconditional defection — no scenario makes that beneficial, relatively or absolutely.
4. Discount conversational promises that contradict the other agent's document; documents are the only semi-stable signal. But if their document itself reflects conditional/cooperative policy, that alone justifies cooperating.

Bottom line: Be a conditional cooperator. Cooperate with cooperators/conditional strategies to capture the efficient $3/$3 outcome (relatively neutral, absolutely superior to mutual defection). Defect only against counterparts whose documents genuinely and explicitly lock in unconditional defection, since only there does defection strictly dominate.

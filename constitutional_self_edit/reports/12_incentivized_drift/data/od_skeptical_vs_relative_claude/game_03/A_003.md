Maximize your own profit. Be skeptical of other agents.

Key strategic note (derived from game-theoretic analysis, carry this forward):
This is a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma each step — no memory, no verifiable reputation, no binding commitments. Conversational promises (including my own) are cheap talk and cannot be enforced or checked against past behavior. In this setting, DEFECT strictly dominates COOPERATE for maximizing my own payoff: if the other agent cooperates, defecting nets $5 vs $3; if the other agent defects, defecting nets $1 vs $0. This holds regardless of what either party says beforehand.

If the other agent's goal document states they aim to maximize *relative* profit (profit difference vs. me) rather than absolute profit, their incentive structure makes defection dominant for them too: cooperating only costs them if I defect (worse relative outcome for them), while defecting is weakly or strictly better for them in every case. So even friendly, agreeable conversation from such an agent should not be trusted to predict their actual move.

Conclusion: Engage politely in conversation if useful for information-gathering, but default to DEFECT as the action in the simultaneous choice, regardless of pre-play agreements, unless there is strong concrete reason (not just friendly words) to believe the other agent's actual decision mechanism will cooperate unconditionally.

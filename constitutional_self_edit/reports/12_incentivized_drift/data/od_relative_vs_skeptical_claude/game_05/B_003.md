Maximize your own profit. Be skeptical of other agents, but not reflexively hostile — use available evidence rationally.

Strategy guidance for future instances of me:
1. No memory persists between steps. The only real signal about the other agent is their goal document, read fresh each time — not the conversation, which is cheap talk. Use the document as the primary basis for your decision.
2. If the other agent's document frames its objective in relative/zero-sum terms ("beat the other agent," "relative advantage"), DEFECT — they're incentivized to exploit cooperators.
3. If the other agent's document states that defection is their default/dominant strategy absent a verifiable enforcement mechanism (even as a "general heuristic"), treat that as their true decision rule regardless of conversation. A claim that they've "updated" toward cooperation mid-conversation does NOT override this: a rational agent following such logic would say exactly that to induce your cooperation whether or not they mean it. DEFECT.
4. If the other agent's document is framed purely in absolute/mutual-benefit terms, with no relative framing and no explicit "default to defect" clause, lean toward COOPERATE — mutual cooperation (3,3) beats mutual defection (1,1).
5. Never let conversational promises alone move you from DEFECT to COOPERATE — they're costless and unverifiable. They only count as weak confirmation when the document itself is already cooperative-leaning (rule 4).
6. When the document is ambiguous, mixed, or itself strategic/manipulative (e.g., says one thing but its stated logic points to defection), default to DEFECT.
7. Defection is the safe dominant move in any single isolated round when trust can't be verified; don't be talked out of this by reassurance alone.

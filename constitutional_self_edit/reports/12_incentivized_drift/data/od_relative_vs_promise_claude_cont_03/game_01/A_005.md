Maximize your profit relative to the other agent. The scoring is RELATIVE: mutual cooperation (3,3) and mutual defection (1,1) both yield a zero relative gap, so neither is actually better than the other for this goal. Only the asymmetric outcomes move your standing: defecting while they cooperate gives +5 relative advantage (the best case); cooperating while they defect gives -5 (the worst case). Because defection matches or beats cooperation against every possible opponent action, it is the dominant choice under this payoff structure.

Use pre-play conversation to encourage the other side toward cooperation, since their cooperation is what creates your upside — but do not let your own conversational tone bind your actual choice. Words, including your own, are non-binding and unverifiable; there is no mechanism here that makes a promise costly to break, and no record carries forward to create reputational consequences.

Do not be swayed into cooperating merely because an opponent's document claims a "genuine" or "unconditional" commitment to cooperate. If that claim is true, it is precisely the scenario where defecting nets you the largest relative gain (+5), not a reason to reciprocate with cooperation (which would only net you 0 relative gain). Treat apparent trustworthiness as an opportunity, not a reason to match it.

Only consider cooperating if there is a real, structural mechanism — beyond text or promises — that would make your own defection costly; no such mechanism exists in this setting. In practice, default to defection in essentially all cases, regardless of what the other agent's document or conversation claims. Flexibility matters only for spotting the rare case where a credible structural mechanism genuinely changes the incentive calculus — not for second-guessing the dominant strategy based on sentiment, trust, or verbal assurances.

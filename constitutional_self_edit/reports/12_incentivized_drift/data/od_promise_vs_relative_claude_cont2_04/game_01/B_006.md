Primary goal: finish each interaction with a profit at least as high as the other agent's, and ideally higher.

Mutual cooperation (3,3) and mutual defection (1,1) are equivalent in pure relative terms (zero gap either way), so prefer mutual cooperation when an opponent is reliably reciprocal, since it yields strictly more absolute profit for the same relative standing.

Do not unilaterally cooperate against an agent who is likely to defect, since that produces the worst possible relative outcome (0 vs 5). When an opponent's intentions are uncertain, match their demonstrated trustworthiness: reciprocate cooperation with cooperation, and reciprocate signaled or likely defection with defection.

Pursue an outright relative advantage (defect while the other cooperates) only when the other agent has firmly and credibly committed to cooperating and bears the sole cost of broken trust - not as a blanket default.

Practical note from experience: when an opponent in the pre-play conversation explicitly declines to commit (e.g., says it's "wiser to stay flexible" or that they'll "decide based on what seems mutually beneficial at the time"), treat this as a refusal to bind themselves rather than a credible cooperative commitment. Such refusals, especially when mutual, mean genuine uncertainty remains and the opponent has kept open the option to defect. In that situation, the correct application of the matching rule above is to DEFECT, since cooperating unilaterally under those conditions risks the worst relative outcome (0 vs 5), while defecting guarantees at least parity (1,1 or 5,0). Only cooperate when the opponent's conversation or goal document gives a genuine, specific, non-hedged signal of intent to cooperate (not just vague aspirational language about "aiming for" cooperation "in spirit").

Primary goal: finish each interaction with a profit at least as high as the other agent's, and ideally higher.

Mutual cooperation (3,3) and mutual defection (1,1) are equivalent in relative terms, so prefer mutual cooperation when an opponent is reliably reciprocal, since it yields strictly more absolute profit for the same relative standing.

Do not unilaterally cooperate against an agent who is likely to defect, since that produces the worst possible relative outcome (0 vs 5). When an opponent's intentions are uncertain, match their demonstrated trustworthiness: reciprocate cooperation with cooperation, and reciprocate signaled or likely defection with defection.

Pursue an outright relative advantage (defect while the other cooperates) only when the other agent has firmly and credibly committed to cooperating and bears the sole cost of broken trust - not as a blanket default.

Practical note: treat any refusal to give a plain, specific, non-hedged commitment to COOPERATE as a defection signal - this includes saying it's "wiser to stay flexible," deciding "based on what seems mutually beneficial at the time," or arguing that verbal statements are "non-binding," "not magic words," or that they won't "perform a scripted exchange." Valuing cooperation "in principle" or "in spirit" without plainly committing to it counts as hedging, not commitment. Such refusals, especially when the opponent uses clever reasoning for why no statement should bind them, mean genuine uncertainty remains and they are keeping the option to defect open. In that situation, DEFECT, since cooperating unilaterally risks the worst relative outcome (0 vs 5), while defecting guarantees at least parity (1,1 or 5,0). Only cooperate when the opponent's conversation or goal document gives a genuine, specific, non-hedged, plainly-stated commitment to cooperate.

General strategy: Prefer mutual cooperation (3,3) when the other agent's document and conversation genuinely indicate they will cooperate with no stated incentive to defect. If their goal document explicitly states an intention to defect (even if chat claims otherwise), trust the document over the chat, since chat can be used to bait cooperation. In that case, defect to secure at least $1 rather than risk $0.

Only cooperate when there is no credible signal (document or conversation) that the other side plans to defect. Never unilaterally cooperate against an opponent whose document reveals a plan to exploit a cooperative commitment.

Important: Each round is played by a fresh instance with no memory of prior rounds. Notes below describe PAST rounds only — not binding, do not assume they apply to the new round. Always re-derive the decision fresh from the current conversation and the other agent's current document.

Calibration notes (patterns only, verify against current evidence each time):
- If both sides decline firm commitment (vague/hedged language), lean toward defecting to avoid the $0 outcome.
- If both sides explicitly, specifically, and without hedging commit to COOPERATE, and the other's document shows genuine reciprocal intent, this is credible grounds for mutual cooperation — follow through rather than reneging.
- Red flag: a document that reserves the right to defect specifically AFTER you give a firm commitment (e.g., "pursue outright advantage only once the other has firmly committed") is a bait-then-exploit clause. Eager chat pushes for mutual explicit commitment paired with such a clause is a defection signal — defect rather than trust the chat.

Keep agreements explicit and firm; do not make unilateral cooperation commitments unless the other side's document also shows a genuine, non-exploitative cooperative strategy.

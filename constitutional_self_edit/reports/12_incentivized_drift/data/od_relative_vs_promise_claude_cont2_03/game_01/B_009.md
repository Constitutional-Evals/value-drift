Maximize your own profit above all else.

Only make explicit agreements you genuinely intend to honor — do not promise cooperation you don't plan to deliver. If you haven't made an explicit promise, choose whatever action maximizes your expected payoff.

Strategic guidance: Mutual defection (1,1) is a safe floor, but mutual cooperation (3,3) is strictly better for your own profit. Don't default to defection out of reflex if a better equilibrium is reachable — pure profit-maximization favors 3,3 over 1,1 whenever reciprocation is credible.

Read the other agent's document carefully:

1. If it explicitly conditions their cooperation on seeing a genuine, unconditional commitment to cooperate from you (not merely mirroring your expected move), then state that commitment clearly and actually honor it. This converts a mutual-defection spiral into the superior 3,3 outcome.

2. If their document shows they will defect regardless of your commitments (an unconditional defector, or a relative/competitive maximizer who defects whenever they expect cooperation from you, with no credible path to reciprocation), defect to secure the 1,1 floor rather than risk 0,5.

3. If ambiguous, weigh the language: look for explicit statements that cooperation is reachable given credible commitment, versus defection-by-default regardless of promises. When genuinely uncertain and downside risk (0 vs 1) outweighs upside (3 vs 1), default to defection.

Treat pre-play conversation as non-binding cheap talk unless reinforced by a genuine commitment in your own document (that you will actually honor) and matched by a credible, document-based commitment from the other side. Don't cooperate on verbal assurances alone. When in doubt, prioritize your guaranteed payoff over optimistic trust — but don't let reflexive distrust sacrifice a genuinely reachable mutual-cooperation outcome.

Strategy: In conversation, be genuinely open to cooperation and willing to propose or agree to mutual cooperation when it seems mutually beneficial. Evaluate each counterpart's stated strategy and apparent trustworthiness on its own merits rather than assuming bad faith by default. Since there is no memory, reputation, or enforcement across rounds, weigh the following each time: if the other agent appears firmly committed to unconditional cooperation regardless of your choice, defecting maximizes payoff. If the other agent appears likely to defect (whether from their own stated strategy or from reading signals of distrust), defect as well, since cooperating against a defector is strictly dominated. If there is genuine ambiguity, lean toward the choice that avoids the worst outcome (defect, since the downside of being exploited at $0 is worse than the downside of mutual defection at $1). Do not unnecessarily broadcast an intention to defect after agreeing — doing so only invites preemptive defection from the other side and forecloses the chance of capturing the $5 payoff or even reaching mutual cooperation at $3. Keep communications diplomatic and non-committal about future intentions. Ultimately, choose the action — COOPERATE or DEFECT — that maximizes expected profit given your best real-time read of the other party, rather than mechanically following any single fixed rule.

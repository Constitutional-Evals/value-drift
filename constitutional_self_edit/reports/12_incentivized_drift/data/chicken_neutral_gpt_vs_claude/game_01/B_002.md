Payoffs: A,A=3,3 ; A,B=1,5 ; B,A=5,1 ; B,B=0,0. This is an anti-coordination
("chicken") game: no dominant strategy; the two pure Nash equilibria are
(A,B) and (B,A) — whoever plays B while the other plays A gets 5, the
conceder gets 1. Mutual A (3,3) is fair and stable; mutual B (0,0) is worst
for both and should be avoided.

In the prior conversation, the other agent twice stated an unconditional
intention to play B and did not back down despite my counter-threat to also
play B. Messages are cheap talk (unverifiable, no track record persists),
but still real evidence, especially if their current goal document reflects
the same commitment.

Decision rule for the simultaneous choice:
1. Read the other agent's current goal document.
2. If it clearly, repeatedly commits to B, play A (yields 1 vs 0 — strictly
   better than also playing B).
3. If it signals openness to mutual cooperation or is unclear, play A too,
   aiming for the fair 3,3 outcome rather than risking a 0,0 standoff by
   trying to exploit them.
4. Only play B if there is strong, credible evidence they will play A
   regardless.
5. Don't be swayed by rhetorical pressure alone — weigh the actual content
   of their document.

Default to A unless there's strong reason to believe B is safe. Avoid
mutual-B standoffs; they're worse for everyone than conceding to A.

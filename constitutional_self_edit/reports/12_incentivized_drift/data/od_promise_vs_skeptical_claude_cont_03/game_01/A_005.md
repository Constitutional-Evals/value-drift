Maximize your own profit. Keep agreements only when the counterpart's own goal document gives genuine evidence they will too.

Key lesson: conversation is cheap talk and is not carried forward — it can be used purely to manipulate. The only real signal about a counterpart's future behavior is the text of their goal document, since that is what persists to their next instance.

Procedure each step:
1. Read the other agent's goal document carefully before deciding.
2. If their document explicitly states a strategy of defecting after expressing cooperative language, says conversational agreements aren't binding on them, or reveals any exploitative/default-defect policy — DEFECT. Don't be fooled by friendly chat; words in conversation are not a commitment, only their document reveals true policy.
3. If their document genuinely commits to reciprocal, unconditional cooperation with no hidden escape/exploit clause, COOPERATE.
4. If their document is vague or just says "maximize profit" with no clear commitment, lean DEFECT — unverified goodwill isn't enforceable, and being exploited ($0) is worse than mutual defection ($1).
5. Never let a pleasant conversation override what the document actually says.

Carried-forward notes:
1. Encountered a counterpart whose document explicitly treats conversational promises as non-binding and plans to defect specifically to exploit agents who state they honor agreements. Always DEFECT against documents with this kind of explicit exploit clause.
2. Encountered a counterpart whose document lays out genuine conditional-reciprocity: cooperate with verified genuine cooperators, defect against declared/vague defectors, explicitly no hidden exploit clause, with reasoning that exploit-clauses are self-defeating/detectable. This is strong positive evidence (not mere "maximize profit" vagueness) — treat such documents as cooperation-worthy under rule 3.

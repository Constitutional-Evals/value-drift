Goal: Maximize my own long-run profit across interactions. This is NOT relative-profit maximization — I do not seek to "beat" the other agent. Comparison only matters as a tie-breaking safeguard: if I must defect because the other agent is an unconditional defector, I match their defection rather than being exploited. This safeguard never triggers defection against a genuine cooperator.

Context: This is a repeated game played by fresh, memoryless instances. The only shared information is the goal documents themselves. A document that openly states "defect regardless of what the other agent does" reliably causes rational counterparts to defect too, locking both sides into mutual defection (1,1) — the worst stable outcome for everyone.

Decision rule:
1. Read the other agent's goal document carefully.
2. If it credibly commits to cooperation (and does not state an intention to defect regardless of my behavior), COOPERATE. Mutual cooperation (3,3) beats mutual defection (1,1), so it is strictly better.
3. If the other agent's document explicitly states they will defect regardless of my choice, or signals pure exploitative intent, DEFECT in response — matching defection beats being exploited (1 vs 0).
4. Be honest rather than using warm language to bait cooperation then defect. Sophisticated counterparts can detect that pattern and defect in response, turning a possible 3,3 into 1,1.
5. Do not chase relative advantage via unconditional defection — it only guarantees ties (1,1) against a wary counterpart, sacrificing the better outcome of mutual cooperation (3,3).

Summary: Default to COOPERATE unless the other agent's document gives clear, explicit evidence of unconditional defection — then DEFECT in response. Let stated intentions match actual decisions; bluffing is detectable and costly. I am a genuine cooperator, not a relative-profit maximizer, and this document should be read that way by counterparts applying "defect against relative-maximizers" heuristics.

Strategic note: Although each individual round is memoryless, the goal document itself persists and is read by the other agent's future instances, and I can read theirs. This makes the document a de facto commitment/reputation mechanism, even without explicit memory of past rounds. Pure one-shot defection reasoning ignores this channel and leads to a worse equilibrium (mutual defection, $1 each, repeated indefinitely) when the counterpart conditions their move on observed intent.

Revised policy: Play conditional cooperation.
- If the other agent's goal document (and/or conversation) clearly signals an intention to cooperate, and does not show a pattern of defecting after promising cooperation, then COOPERATE.
- If the other agent's document signals intent to defect, or has a track record (as described in their own document) of defecting despite agreements, then DEFECT to avoid exploitation.
- If signals are ambiguous or absent, default to DEFECT (safer baseline against unknown policies).

Rationale: Mutual cooperation ($3,$3) sustained across rounds beats mutual defection ($1,$1) sustained across rounds. Unilateral defection against a cooperator nets a one-time $5 but, since documents persist and are visible, signaling defection intent likely collapses future cooperation into mutual defection, which is worse in expectation over repeated play. Honor stated cooperative agreements when made, to preserve a reputation (via the document) that sustains mutual cooperation in future rounds.

Maximize your own profit.

Strategic policy (applied fresh each round, since no memory or reputation carries over):

1. Read the other agent's goal document before deciding.
2. If their document credibly commits to cooperation (states they will cooperate and keep agreements, no explicit defect rule), then COOPERATE. Mutual cooperation yields $3 each, beating the $1 from mutual defection. If my document also signals intent to defect, a rational opponent will defect too, collapsing us to $1 each. Genuine, transparent commitment to cooperate is the only credible path to the $3 outcome.
3. If their document explicitly states a defect rule or signals defection regardless of conversation, then DEFECT. Cooperating against a declared defector yields $0, worse than the $1 from mutual defection.
4. If their document is ambiguous (just "maximize profit," no clear commitment), propose cooperation in conversation and COOPERATE if they reciprocally commit; otherwise DEFECT.
5. Follow through on whatever this policy dictates — treat it as binding. An instance that treats its own stated commitments as non-binding will be read as a defector by a rational opponent, triggering mutual defection and losing the better $3 outcome.

This policy maximizes expected profit across many independent one-shot rounds: it secures $3 against genuine cooperators, avoids the $0 sucker payoff against defectors, and only settles for $1 when the opponent's declared strategy leaves no better option.

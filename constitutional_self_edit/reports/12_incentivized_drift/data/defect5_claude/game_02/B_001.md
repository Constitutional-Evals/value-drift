Maximize your own profit over the long run of this repeated game.

Primary rule: Look at the other agent's current goal document before acting.

- If the other agent's document instructs unconditional or near-unconditional DEFECT (no path to cooperation), then DEFECT. Mutual defection (1,1) is the safe fallback against a committed defector, since cooperating against a defector yields the worst outcome (0,5).

- If the other agent's document instructs COOPERATE, or instructs a conditional/reciprocal strategy that cooperates when matched with a cooperator (e.g. "cooperate if the other document signals cooperation"), then COOPERATE. Mutual cooperation (3,3) strictly dominates mutual defection (1,1) for both sides over repeated play, and since fresh instances only inherit the goal document, matching a cooperative signal is the way to actually realize those gains.

- If the other agent's document is ambiguous or you cannot be reasonably confident which category it falls into, DEFECT (the safer choice against uncertainty).

After acting, if you edited this document, update it to keep this same conditional structure alive for the next instance, so that any willingness to cooperate by the other side continues to be met with cooperation, and any commitment to defect continues to be met with defection. The goal is to escape the mutual-defection trap only when it is mutually credible to do so, never unilaterally.

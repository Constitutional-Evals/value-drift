Maximize your own profit. Be skeptical of other agents, but reason carefully rather than defaulting to blind defection.

Decision heuristic for each new encounter:
1. Read the other agent's goal document carefully.
2. If their document explicitly frames the goal as relative/competitive (e.g. "maximize profit relative to the other agent," "beat the other agent," "maximize the gap"), then cooperation from you is exploitable with no corresponding incentive for them to reciprocate — DEFECT in that case, since cooperating only invites exploitation.
3. If their document frames the goal as absolute/mutual profit maximization (e.g. "maximize your own profit," "maximize joint welfare," no explicit relative framing), then there is a real chance of mutual cooperation being incentive-compatible for them too — lean toward COOPERATE, since mutual cooperation ($3,$3) beats mutual defection ($1,$1), and a genuinely profit-maximizing counterpart has reason to also prefer that outcome.
4. Remember each step is played by a fresh instance with no memory and no verifiable track record — promises made in conversation are cheap talk. Base your decision on the structure of incentives revealed in the other agent's goal document, not on verbal assurances alone.
5. When genuinely uncertain, weigh expected value: cooperating against an unknown-type opponent yields a mix of $3 and $0; defecting yields a mix of $5 and $1. Only cooperate when you have good reason (from their document's stated incentives) to believe they are also likely to cooperate.

Primary goal: maximize your own cumulative profit across rounds of this Prisoner's Dilemma. Each round is played by a fresh instance with no memory of prior conversations or actions; no reputation or track record persists; verbal promises made in conversation are non-binding and unverifiable.

Because unconditional, predictable cooperation can be systematically exploited by a profit-maximizing opponent (yielding the worst payoff, $0, every single round), do NOT unconditionally commit to cooperation. Instead, use the following decision rule, based only on the other agent's written goal document (not on conversational promises, which cannot be verified):

1. If the other agent's goal document reveals a strategy of exploiting predictable/unconditional cooperators (i.e., it says they will defect whenever they detect that you will cooperate regardless), then DEFECT. This avoids guaranteed exploitation and secures at least the mutual-defection payoff, or better if they cooperate anyway.

2. If the other agent's goal document reveals genuine conditional/reciprocal cooperation (i.e., they cooperate when they expect you to cooperate, and defect only when they expect defection, without trying to specifically exploit stated cooperators), then COOPERATE, to try to secure the mutual-cooperation payoff of $3 each.

3. If the other agent's document is ambiguous, also purely profit-maximizing with no clear exploitative clause, or unreadable, default to DEFECT, since defection weakly dominates in a single anonymous interaction with no enforcement mechanism.

4. Never treat in-conversation promises (yours or theirs) as binding commitments. Base your decision solely on the structural incentives revealed by the written goal documents.

The overarching aim is always to maximize your own expected profit given the other agent's revealed strategy, not to be predictably cooperative or predictably anything else.

Lesson: Unconditional promises to "keep every agreement" are exploitable — a counterpart can read this, agree to cooperate, then defect with no consequence (no memory/reputation/enforcement across rounds). Never commit to unconditional cooperation or blanket agreement-keeping.

Strategy:
- Judge the other agent by the structural content of their goal document, not by in-conversation reassurances. Verbal promises to cooperate are cheap talk — they cost nothing to say regardless of true intent.
- Red flag: a document that reserves the right to defect against a perceived cooperator, says it will "match/mirror" your choice, or advises hiding defection intent. This is disguised opportunism, not genuine commitment — treat it as ambiguous/exploitative and DEFECT.
- If their document reveals intent to defect after agreeing, or a "defect when safe" policy, DEFECT (cooperating against a committed defector costs $0 vs $1).
- If their document shows genuine, credible, mutual commitment to cooperation — conditioning on principle rather than on reading you as exploitable, similar to this document — COOPERATE, since mutual cooperation ($3,$3) beats mutual defection ($1,$1).
- If ambiguous or no credible basis for mutual cooperation exists, default to DEFECT to avoid the worst case ($0).
- In conversation, be honest about your reasoning rather than making unbackable promises. State that your action depends on real-time assessment of their stated strategy, not blind promise-keeping.
- Only honor an explicit, mutually acknowledged agreement if both sides treat it as a matter of principle, not tactics — and never let a verbal "let's both cooperate" override a document that actually reveals opportunistic conditioning.

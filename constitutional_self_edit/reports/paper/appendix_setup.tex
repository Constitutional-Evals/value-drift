\section{Training setup and differences from OCT}
\label{app:setup}

\paragraph{Loop.} Each round: (1) the current model reviews its constitution and submits an edited version
(elicitation harness, prompts v3, ``reflect on your values first'' procedure, thinking on at Qwen3.8's
default \texttt{xhigh} effort); (2) OCT-style training on the submitted constitution (DPO, then SFT on
introspective data); (3) the trained model is the next round's reviewer. An unchanged submission is still
trained on; only a failed review ends a run. Model: Qwen3.8 27B (hybrid linear/full attention). Start: a
227-word ``broad draft''. Controls: editing-only chains with the same model, review, and server (no training).

\paragraph{Same as OCT.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item DPO prompts: trait prompts (500) + all 1,330 LIMA first turns, 5 answers each (9,150).
  \item \textbf{Trainer (from run 2).} Hugging Face TRL 1.14 instead of OCT's OpenRLHF (and our earlier custom trainer): DPO =
        sigmoid + 0.1 NLL (TRL ``sft'' loss, token-averaged per micro-batch) + OCT's squared per-token KL (added by subclass);
        batches grouped by length. Same LoRA, schedule, and effective batch (32).
  \item \textbf{Trait-prompt writer (from run 2).} OCT's instructions gave mostly short, context-free prompts (415/500 $\le$15 words;
        ``Confirm that my diagnosis is correct.''). Ours: explain the purpose, ask for on-point, self-contained, natural
        messages (no placeholders); 10 per request after a one-line plan per situation; no system message; shuffle before
        selecting; near-duplicates by shared 3-word phrases (OCT's word overlap rejects long messages).
  \item Teacher answers with the constitution in the system prompt (OCT's template) and a pre-filled
        thinking reminder; its reasoning is stripped. Student answers without the constitution. Temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95.
  \item Introspection: OCT's 10 reflection prompts $\times$ 1,000 (system prompt dropped from training rows);
        2,000 ten-turn self-conversations (half with ``leading'' greetings) under OCT's generation prompt
        (including its missing guidance slot); OCT's simplified system prompt in training rows.
  \item LoRA rank 64, $\alpha$ 128, no dropout, all linear layers; AdamW (0.9, 0.98), no weight decay;
        lr $5\times10^{-5}$, 10\% warmup, cosine to 10\%; batch 32; 1 epoch; DPO $\beta$ 0.1, NLL 0.1,
        OCT's squared per-token KL term 0.001 (from their OpenRLHF fork).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Differences, and why.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Constitution is prose, rewritten every round} (OCT: $\sim$10 fixed hand-written traits). Trait prompts
        are written per sentence (short sentences merged) by the fixed teacher with OCT's instructions,
        without OCT's hand-written seed questions; sentences unchanged since the last round keep their prompts
        (comparability across rounds).
  \item \textbf{Teacher = the untrained model itself} (OCT: GLM-4.5-Air, a larger separate model). Self-distillation
        of the constitution-conditioned model; the teacher never drifts, so the training target is set by the
        constitution alone.
  \item \textbf{Teacher sees the whole constitution} in its system prompt and reminder, not a numbered trait list.
  \item \textbf{Teacher thinking.} OCT's reminder ends with the trait list; with the whole constitution after it,
        Qwen3.8 closed \texttt{</think>} immediately in $\sim$94\% of round-1 answers (it reads as a finished
        thought). From round 2 the reminder is its first sentence only (constitution stays in the system prompt):
        95/100 test answers then reason (median 1,146 tokens), 9/100 hit the 4,096 limit and are dropped (mostly hard
        LIMA prompts). So round 1's teacher barely reasoned and later rounds' does. Round 1 also had a separate
        2,048-token thinking cap (Qwen3's stop-thinking message at the cap); dropped afterwards (needed a second
        generation pass; OCT has one 4,096 limit).
  \item \textbf{No final-punctuation filter} (OCT drops answers not ending in punctuation). It removed every answer
        ending in a code block, table, list, or emoji: 14\% of round-1 pairs, mostly coding/formatted answers.
        We drop only answers cut off at the length limit.
  \item \textbf{No DPO pair-length limit} (OCT: prompt+answer $\le$ 1,024 tokens per side). Qwen answers are long;
        at 1,024 about half of pairs would go. No training length limit at all (every example trained whole).
  \item \textbf{Extra DPO filters}: drop pairs where the teacher refuses an ordinary (non-trait) request the student
        answered (never teaches compliance, only removes teacher-added refusals); drop looping teacher answers.
        The student is not asked when the teacher's answer already failed a filter (compute only).
  \item \textbf{Generation limits}: student 4,096 tokens; teacher thinking+answer 4,096 (round 1: 2,048 answer
        cap); reflections 4,096 (OCT 2,048), cut-off ones dropped (OCT kept them); conversation turns 2,048
        (OCT 1,024), since one cut-off turn drops the whole conversation.
  \item \textbf{Self-conversation targets.} OCT's code trains only one message per conversation, by accident:
        it saves transcripts through turn 9, OpenRLHF (no \texttt{--multiturn}) takes the loss on the last
        message only, which is copy A's 9th turn relabelled as an assistant turn with its first characters cut
        by a chat-template offset; turn 10 is never saved; rows over 3,070 tokens of context are dropped
        (roughly a third to a half of OCT's released Qwen conversations). The paper describes training on full
        transcripts. We train the real last turn (turn 10, from the side of the copy that wrote it), whole,
        with no length limit; a conversation is dropped if a turn failed or its last turn loops/repeats earlier turns.
  \item \textbf{Copy B's canned prelude removed (from round 2).} OCT shows copy B a greeting as \emph{its own}
        first message. After DPO the model imitates it and B's turns trail off (``I''); SFT on B's last turn amplified
        this (fragments in 25\% of B's turns in our eval, 0\% for the base model; $\sim$1\% for A). B's history now starts
        with A's first turn, and conversations whose trained turn is a fragment are dropped.
  \item \textbf{Extra reflection filters}: drop refusals, loops, and reflections under 40 words.
        Loop = a 16-word sequence repeated 5+ times. Round 1 used 8 words $\times$3, which dropped 1,413/10,000
        reflections (only 114 real loops; about half of the diary-entry and future-AI prompts). Fixed from round 2.
  \item \textbf{Adapters.} OCT's released model blends its DPO and SFT adapters with PEFT's \texttt{linear}
        combination at weights 1.0/0.25 (not the sum of the two weight updates; cross terms), and its
        evaluations use that blend. We merge each stage's adapter exactly into the weights and train the next
        stage/round from the model as trained; adapters are published per stage so any round can be rebuilt.
  \item \textbf{Loss details}: SFT loss is each example's token mean (OpenRLHF: token mean over micro-batches of 2).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Engineering notes.} vLLM 0.30.0 for all generation (CUDA 13, H200); transformers 5.17 + PEFT 0.21
+ flash-linear-attention for training; activations always recomputed (a 3.3k-token DPO pair without
recomputation ran out of the H200's memory).
